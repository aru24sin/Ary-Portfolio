Case study 02 · 2026 · Independent product

Sfumo

A focus tool is only as good as what it leaves out.

A macOS menu bar app that blurs everything except the window you're using. I designed, built, branded and launched it on my own.

Visit the product site ↗
Role
  • Product designer, developer, brand
Team
  • Solo, with AI pair-programming
Timeline
  • October 2026 · v1.0 live
Tools
  • Swift, SwiftUI, HTML & CSS, Stripe
ProblemWhy it matters

The window I was using was never the only thing I saw.

Full-screen mode hides your work and window managers rearrange it. I wanted the clutter to go quiet without anything closing or moving.

Final productIn use

Everything stays open. One thing stays sharp.

Decision 01Trust

Ask for nothing.

No account, no network, and none of the macOS permission prompts that make people hesitate. That promise now leads the product page.

Instead ofThe Accessibility permission, which is easier to build with

Decision 02Motion

One transition, not two.

Only the background cross-fades when you switch windows. I built a second animation for the incoming window, saw it blink, and cut it.

Instead ofAnimating the focused window too

Decision 03Brand

As quiet as the product.

Warm paper, serif type, and a hero that is a working demo: click a window and the blur follows.

Instead ofA dark, glowing landing page

ImpactResults
0permissions requested
$5one-time, live now
Soloidea to checkout

A complete product where I made every call: what it does, what it refuses to do, what it costs and how it explains itself.

Sfumo launched this month, so there are no sales or usage numbers to quote yet.

ReflectionWhat I learned

I fixed a real bug that wasn't the one I was looking at. Now I pin down exactly what looks wrong before I change anything.

Key takeaway

My best decisions here were things I removed.

A permission prompt, a second animation, a louder brand. I can take a product from idea to checkout alone, and I know when to stop adding.
